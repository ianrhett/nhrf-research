PART B: THE 19 STATUS CLAIMS (full text behind each one-pager row)
For each, split the text into atomic claims (every bill number, date, dollar figure, acreage, percentage, name and absence claim) and check each one. Also judge whether the status label (Implemented / Partly implemented / No implementation reported) fits the verified facts.

P01 Keep building off steep slopes, and keep the slopes wooded. Partly implemented. "Since 2016, rural subdivision rules protect slopes over 15% when land is subdivided in rural policy areas. Countywide, residential grading limits still start at 20%, lots of an acre or more are exempt, and the 15% rule NashvilleNext asked Metro to begin work on within 1 to 3 years (2015) has not been enacted."

P02 Preserve the Highland Rim escarpment as a natural landscape. Partly implemented. "Rural and Conservation policy and the 2016 rural subdivision rules cover much of the Rim, but the policies "are not regulatory" and the Rim has never been mapped or protected by name. In 2025 Council recognized it as "Nashville's Highland Rim Forest" (recognition only; no boundary, rules or funding); a Highland Rim Forests master plan has been in the capital budget since 2024, "Not started.""

P03 Keep sewers and urban services out of rural Rim land. Partly implemented. "The rural no-sewer policy is applied when land is rezoned (Cato Road upzonings denied 2022 and 2023), but Metro Water decides sewer extensions on capacity and developer funding with no rural-policy check, and Council keeps accepting new sewer mains in the Whites Creek corridor. The Alliance reports an extension under way to the Best Brands warehouse; records requested."

P04 Keep rural land rural and low density. Partly implemented. "Rural policy is applied through 2016 rural subdivision rules, downzonings and denied upzonings, but binding tools stop at subdivision, and the recommended agricultural zoning category was never created."

P05 Require conservation subdivisions that keep open space. Partly implemented. "Recommended by right with 50% open space (2011, 2015); a 2006 bill was withdrawn. The 2022 Conservation Development rule keeps 20%, is mandatory only in R and RS zones, and does not reach AR2a or AG land."

P06 Protect existing trees on private land during development. Partly implemented. "2022 tree rules protect retained trees and require replacement once land is under development review, but nothing requires keeping existing trees; the 2022 canopy retention bill was withdrawn in 2023."

P07 Hold the county's tree canopy, including large forested tracts. Partly implemented. "Canopy grew 5,247 acres 2010-2021 but fell about 900 acres 2016-2021, all in the urban district; rural canopy grew and is above its goal. Planting is funded, but no rule protects large forested tracts on private land."

P08 Buy and protect natural land on and around the Rim. Partly implemented. "Metro buys Rim land when chances arise (Beaman Park 2015; about 300 acres announced in 2026, transfer pending closing; easements 2009) but has no acquisition program or land bank. Metro reports $12.8 million allocated for park land since 2017, against a 2017 estimated market value of $534 million for recommended land; the two are different measures. A $7 million Highland Rim conservation easement project is listed in the adopted FY2027 capital budget (27DS0031, $7,000,000, requested by At-Large CM Burkley Allen; adopted CIB p III-91); FY2027 Capital Spending Plan not yet issued as of 2026-11-02."

P09 Meet the conserved-land acreage goals. Partly implemented. "Parkland grew (about 11,000 acres in 2011 to 15,657 in 2025 on Metro figures, which conflict), but Plan to Play's 4,541-acre target for 2026 is not yet due (568.78 acres reported; about 300 acres next to Beaman Park pending closing), and the 22,000-acre, private, regulatory and agricultural goals were never reported against."

P10 Keep the Open Space Fund replenished. No implementation reported. "No record found in FY2017 to FY2026 Capital Spending Plans names the Open Space Fund or its balance (searched 2026-11-02: every Capital Spending Plan file on the Finance capital budget page, FY2018 to FY2026 exhibits, resolutions, memos and presentation, and the Plan to Play 2026 Appendix; FY2017 has no spending plan file on that page); land money arrives in irregular capital allocations, zero in most years."

P11 Use conservation easements and transfer of development rights. Partly implemented. "Metro used easements once (290 acres, 2009) and has a 2017 grant fund that can pay for them, but no grants were found, the FY2027 Highland Rim conservation easement project is listed in the adopted FY2027 capital budget (27DS0031, $7,000,000, requested by At-Large CM Burkley Allen; adopted CIB p III-91); FY2027 Capital Spending Plan not yet issued as of 2026-11-02, and no transfer of development rights exists for natural land."

P12 Join regional efforts to conserve upstream land. No implementation reported. "No Metro agreement or program for upstream or regional land conservation was found. The 2015 action names regional partners as implementers, not a Metro department."

P13 Designate and actively manage Metro's natural areas. Partly implemented. "The Park Board adopted a Designated Natural Areas policy on August 4, 2026, with the first 14 sites on its September agenda; site management plans, a target share and a Council ordinance do not exist yet."

P14 Run a funded urban forestry program and keep canopy studies current. Implemented. "Urban forestry staff and five-year canopy studies are in the Metro Code (2021), and a dedicated tree fund of up to $2.5 million a year is permanent (2026)."

P15 Protect rare habitats and species. Partly implemented. "Stream buffers have protected aquatic habitat since the late 1990s; no Metro rule was found for the habitat overlay, wetland mitigation, cedar glades or invasive species asked for."

P16 Protect scenic hills, vistas and roadside corridors. Partly implemented. "About 120 miles of roads are tagged Scenic, with scenic easements required when land along them is subdivided; scenic hills and vistas were never identified or protected."

P17 Site development around existing trees, land forms and natural features. Partly implemented. "Tree surveys and root-zone protection are binding, but compliance is measured by replacement density, and site-design guidance is not regulatory."

P18 Assemble a connected system of protected natural areas and open space. Partly implemented. "Land is added piece by piece and a $1.01 million Ecological Conservation Strategy is under way, but there is no adopted, mapped system of protected natural areas."

P19 Broad goals to protect and invest in the natural environment. No implementation reported. "The goals have no measurable target; NashvilleNext progress reports stop after 2016, and the climate plan's green-space baseline is due in FY2027."
