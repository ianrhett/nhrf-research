## Claim text under review (what_was_done)

> 2022 tree rules protect retained trees and require replacement once land is under development review, but nothing requires keeping existing trees; the 2022 canopy retention bill was withdrawn in 2023.

## Atomic claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Source and cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2022 tree rules protect retained trees | VERIFIED | BL2022-1122 Substitute (passed third reading as amended 2022-05-17, effective 2022-05-27; Legistar matter 14204), 17.28.065.D: "To assure the health and survival of retained trees", listed injuries are prohibited, and "A circular tree protection zone shall be established around each retained tree." Retained trees are inspected one year after completion. Root-zone rules updated by BL2024-687 (effective 2025-03-07). |
| 2 | ...and require replacement | VERIFIED | BL2022-1122, 17.28.065.C: "Trees removed pursuant to Section 17.40.470, tree removal permit procedures, shall be replaced at the expense of the property owner or developer to meet the required tree density standard"; "Any retained tree that is removed without a tree removal permit shall be replaced." Density targets: 22 units per acre non-residential, 14 units per acre in new single and two-family subdivisions, "using retained or replacement trees, or a combination of both." |
| 3 | ...once land is under development review | VERIFIED, needs wording | The density and protection duties attach to development activity, site plans and permits (17.28.065; subdivision density proof "prior to the release of any applicable public infrastructure bonds"). The ordinance does not use the phrase "development review"; "once land is being developed under a permit or plan" is closer. Not checked: whether by-right single-family building permits on existing lots trigger the duties (the substitute has a lot-frontage tree rule for individual lots other than AG and AR2a, MPC minutes 2022-04-28, PDF p26). |
| 4 | ...but nothing requires keeping existing trees | CORRECTED | Mostly right, with one exception in the same ordinance, 17.28.065.A.3: "No historic or specimen tree shall be removed without a finding by the Metropolitan Beautification and Environment Commission that the tree is a hazard or a determination that it is not economically or practically feasible to develop the parcel without removing the tree." That program is voluntary and needs owner consent. Outside it, the rules set an intent to "minimize the removal of trees" and a design duty to "attempt to preserve" protected and heritage trees (17.28.065.B.1), and density can be met with replacements. Say "no general rule requires keeping existing trees." |
| 5 | The 2022 canopy retention bill was withdrawn in 2023 | VERIFIED | BL2022-1409 (matter 14985), "An ordinance amending Title 2 and Title 17 of the Metropolitan Code of Laws relative to trees": filed 2022-08-30; Planning Commission "recommended for withdrawal" 2023-02-23; withdrawn 2023-03-07 (Legistar history). The attached text sets a "Minimum Tree Canopy Retention" table by transect and "Tree Save Areas" (Ordinance Text, PDF p14), so "canopy retention bill" is accurate. |

## Proposed text for operator ruling

> Since 2022, trees kept on a development site must be fenced and protected, and removed trees must be replaced to meet a tree density standard (BL2022-1122). No general rule requires keeping existing trees; only trees enrolled by their owners in the Historic and Specimen Tree program need a finding before removal. A 2022 bill that would have required keeping a share of existing canopy was withdrawn in March 2023 (BL2022-1409).
