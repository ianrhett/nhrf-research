## Claim text under review (what_was_done)

> Tree surveys and root-zone protection are binding, but compliance is measured by replacement density, and site-design guidance is not regulatory.

## Atomic claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Source and cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tree surveys are binding | VERIFIED, needs scope | BL2022-1122 Substitute (passed third reading as amended 2022-05-17, effective 2022-05-27; Legistar matter 14204), new 17.28.065: trees of 24 inches DBH or more, or heritage trees, "shall be survey located and depicted on the final site plan"; "All retained trees, pursuant to a tree survey shall be inspected by the urban forester one year following completion of the project." The survey duty applies to sites under development review, and to large and heritage trees, not to every tree. |
| 2 | Root-zone protection is binding | VERIFIED | BL2022-1122, 17.28.065.D: "A circular tree protection zone shall be established around each retained tree", at least ten feet or the full drip line. BL2024-687 (passed third reading as amended 2025-03-04, effective 2025-03-07; matter 18396) replaced 17.28.065.D: "A tree protection zone shall be established around each retained tree, tree preservation area, and natural area, as required", made of a Structural Root Zone and a critical root zone. Protection covers retained trees only; it does not require that any tree be retained. |
| 3 | Compliance is measured by replacement density | VERIFIED, needs wording | BL2022-1122, 17.28.065: non-residential properties "shall attain a tree density factor of at least twenty-two units per acre using retained or replacement trees, or a combination of both"; new single and two-family subdivisions "at least fourteen units per acre using retained or replacement trees, or a combination of both, excluding the area of the building lots." Compliance is measured by tree density, which can be met with retained trees, replacement trees or both. "Replacement density" overstates it; "tree density, which replacement trees can meet" is exact. |
| 3a | Context missing | FLAG | Same section, 17.28.065.B.1: "It is the intent of this section to minimize the removal of trees and to ensure that property owners and developers take reasonable measures to design and locate the proposed improvements so that the number of trees to be removed is minimized. In particular, the design shall attempt to preserve protected, heritage, and specimen and historic trees." This is binding text that speaks directly to siting around trees, but it is an intent and an "attempt" duty, not a retention standard. The row omits it; the implementation notes mention it. |
| 4 | Site-design guidance is not regulatory | VERIFIED | NashvilleNext Vol. III Community Character Manual, Introduction (amended 2017), PDF p25: "Given that the Community Character Policies are not regulatory, the descriptions in each policy do not use verbs such as 'shall.'" PDF p14: Community Plans are policy documents, "While not regulatory, like zoning, they are used as the basis for guiding some regulations, such as rezoning or subdivision of property." The site-design statements that make up P17 come from these policies (CCM CI/CO/OS/T PDF p9, p19, p21; T2 PDF p2; T3 PDF p1, per `data/recommendation_statements.csv`). |

## Omission found

The row cites only tree rules. Binding siting rules for land forms and natural features also exist and belong to this recommendation: BL2022-1121 conservation development keeps natural areas in open space and bars clearing them (see P05), and the 2016 Rural Character Subdivision rules protect Primary Conservation Land (see P01, P04). The implementation notes list these (BL2022-1121 17.12.090.E.2; Subdivision Regulations 3-3.4, 4-1.1.e, 4-2.6); the row text does not.

Partly implemented = part of it, or a weaker form. No implementation reported = no record found either way, or no measure.

Binding rules require surveys of large trees, protection zones for retained trees and a design intent to minimize removal, but the measured standard can be met by planting replacements, and the broader site-design guidance is policy. That is a weaker form.

Old status: Partly implemented. Proposed status: Partly implemented (no change).

## Proposed text for operator ruling

> Since 2022, sites under development review must survey large and heritage trees, design to "minimize" tree removal, and fence a protection zone around each retained tree (BL2022-1122; root-zone rules updated by BL2024-687 in 2025). Compliance is measured by tree density, which retained or replacement trees can meet. Conservation and rural subdivision rules keep some natural features in open space. The Community Character Manual's site-design guidance is "not regulatory."
